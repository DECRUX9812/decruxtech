Who this is for

Built for the businesses that keep Saskatchewan running.

Small firms here share the same handful of failure modes, and each industry has one that bites first. Below is what we see, and the first thing worth fixing in each.

01

The six we know properly.

We would rather go deep in six places than claim expertise in twenty.

1 to 50 people
Regina and area
01 / Clinics

Health and dental

Patient records, booking, and an inbox full of things that look like invoices. Saskatchewan clinics answer to HIPA, not PIPEDA, which changes what you are allowed to do with data and what you must be able to prove.

  • Who can see patient data, and how that is logged
  • Whether the practice management system is backed up properly
  • MFA on the accounts that can reach records
02 / Trades

Electrical, plumbing, mechanical

Work happens in trucks and basements, often out of signal. The failures are practical: quotes lost in a phone, invoices sent twice, photos scattered across three phones.

  • One place for jobs, quotes and photos
  • Phones and tablets that survive a jobsite
  • Email that does not get your domain blacklisted
03 / Professional

Accounting, legal, consulting

Client files are the whole business, and a breach is a professional-liability event, not an inconvenience. The usual gap is not malware, it is access that was never removed.

  • Former staff who still have a login
  • Client documents outside the approved system
  • Email security that catches invoice fraud
04 / Nonprofits

Charities and associations

Donor records, grant deadlines, and a volunteer who set up the website in 2019 and has since moved away. Small budgets, high turnover, and nobody holding the keys.

  • Donor data you can export and keep
  • Access shared by role, not by password text message
  • A site someone can update without a developer
05 / Retail

Shops and suppliers

Point of sale, inventory, and a card terminal that must never be the reason a customer walks. Downtime is measured in sales, not tickets.

  • Network separated from the staff Wi-Fi and cameras
  • Restore plan for the POS config
  • Offline fallback for payments and receipts
06 / Hospitality

Restaurants and cafés

Booking, delivery apps, staff turnover, and a shared tablet behind the bar that everyone uses for everything. Cheap to fix early, expensive to untangle later.

  • Separate accounts per person, not one shared login
  • Reservations and delivery reconciled in one view
  • Wi-Fi for guests isolated from the till
Not on the list? The work is usually the same shape. If you are between one and fifty people, in Saskatchewan, and computers matter to your day, it is worth a conversation.
02

The same three failures, everywhere.

Across all six, the expensive problems are almost never technical novelty. They are ordinary neglect with a long fuse.

Seen repeatedly

Nobody owns it

Three people each assume someone else renews the licence, checks the backup, or removed the old staff account.

Nobody wrote it down

The system works, but only in one person's head. When they leave, so does the knowledge.

Nobody tested it

The backup runs nightly. Whether it restores has never been checked, which is the only part that matters.
