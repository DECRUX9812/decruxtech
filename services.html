01

Managed IT. The part you stop thinking about.

Most of what small firms call IT problems are actually coordination problems: a licence nobody renewed, a laptop nobody set up, a password stuck in someone's head. Managed IT is the standing arrangement that keeps those from becoming your afternoon.

Support
Users · devices · vendors

One route for every issue, a human on the other end of it, and a documented answer within one business day.

We take the whole surface: onboarding and offboarding, device procurement and setup, Microsoft 365 admin, printers that only work on Tuesdays, and the ten vendors you never wanted. You stop being the person who knows which cable goes where.

What actually changes

  • There is one inbox and one number for anything technical.
  • New staff arrive to a working desk, not a to-do list.
  • Departing staff lose access the same day they leave.
  • You get a written record of what exists and who has access to it.

Suited to firms of one to fifty people, where there is no IT department and no appetite for building one.

Scope
Users, devices, accounts, vendors, line-of-business apps

Single helpdesk inbox and phone, human reply within one business day
Cadence

Documentation and admin access stay yours, in your tenant

Month to month, cancel anytime
On leaving: if you ever move on, you take the documentation, the licences and the admin account. That is deliberate. Lock-in is a bad business model.
02

Cyber defence. You should be able to explain it.

Security theatre is easy: buy a dashboard, screenshot it, sleep badly. We work the other way round, starting with the three things that actually cost small firms money, then writing down what is left.

Identity · backups
Recovery

CyberShield Review

The first engagement, and often the only one needed to sleep properly. We look at how access is granted, how data is backed up, and what would happen tomorrow if the main machine did not start.

  • MFA enforced on email and every admin account, verified rather than assumed
  • A real restore test, not a green tick in a vendor portal
  • Account and licence inventory: who exists, what they can reach
  • A fix-first list, ordered by what it would cost you if ignored

You leave with a short document. It says what is exposed, what it would take to fix, and what we would do first.

Standing

Identity first

Almost every incident starts with a password. MFA and a clean account list stop the ordinary version of that story.

Standing

Backups that restore

An untested backup is a rumour. We test the restore and write down how long it took.

Standing

A path for bad days

Who to call, what to unplug, what to say to clients. Written before you need it.

03

Web and automation. Technology that earns.

A website is either bringing you work or quietly costing you credibility. Automation is either saving hours or generating new chores. We only build the versions that earn their place.

Conversion
Handoff

Websites

Fast, clear, and written for the person deciding whether to call you. Usually that means fewer pages, fewer claims, and a great deal more specificity about what you actually do.

  • Rebuild or repair, depending on what is underneath
  • Copy written with you, not lorem-ipsum filler
  • Forms that arrive somewhere a human reads
  • Speed and accessibility treated as part of the build

Automation

One workflow per sprint. We pick the task that eats the most hours, build the smallest thing that removes it, and hand it over running, with a manual fallback.

  • Intake, quoting, scheduling, reminders, records
  • Built on infrastructure you control, or ours
  • Documented, with a way to switch it off
  • Measured before and after, in hours
